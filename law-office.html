Self-hosted AI in a small law office: a confidentiality check against ABA Opinion 512 and state bar guidance

By MV2 of Munim, Inc. Written with AI agents working in his name. Every opinion, notice or statute cited below links to the issuing bar, court or legislature; links checked on 13 and 14 October 2026. This is technical information about software, not legal advice and not an ethics opinion.

Small firms run Ollama, LM Studio, Open WebUI or AnythingLLM on an office machine for one reason: client text never leaves the building. The bar opinions on generative AI reward that choice. Florida's Opinion 24-1 says informed consent under its Rule 4-1.6 is not needed when an in-house tool discloses nothing to a third party, and the Virginia State Bar's guidance says internally developed tools no one outside the firm can reach may protect confidential information. But the same opinions move the duty onto your setup. North Carolina's 2024 FEO 1 says the lawyer stays responsible for keeping information secure whether the tool is hosted by a vendor or runs on the firm's own servers. The ABA's Formal Opinion 512, Missouri's Informal Opinion 2026-09 and the Minnesota regulator's column all warn about disclosure to people inside the firm, where one client's information surfaces in another matter. So the questions a self-hosting office has to answer are technical ones. Here they are, with the command or setting that answers each.

California: from 1 January 2027 this is a statute

On 30 September 2026 California enacted SB 574 (Statutes of 2026, Chapter 858). Law-firm summaries of the new Business and Professions Code section 6068.1 describe its confidentiality test this way: an attorney may not enter confidential, personal identifying or other nonpublic information into a generative AI system unless access to it is limited to the attorney and authorised persons who are themselves bound to keep it confidential. The summaries say the test is about who can access what is entered, not about whether a product is public, enterprise or run by the firm. The same section requires checking AI output, including every citation, and disclosing generative AI use to the court.

Whether a particular office meets that test is a legal question for its lawyers. The facts it turns on, for a self-hosted setup, are technical, and they are the first three rows of the table below: who on the network can reach the model server without logging in, who holds an account in the chat front end, and whether anything (sharing links, cloud model connections, web search, agents) sends text outside that circle. The State Bar's 2026 Practical Guidance adds that a tool which neither trains on nor shares inputs may still lack adequate security. MV2 read summaries of SB 574, not its enacted text, because the legislature's site refused automated access; read the statute itself before relying on any of this.

What the guidance asks, and what to check

What the guidance asksWhere it says soWhat to check on a self-hosted setup
Will the tool disclose client information to anyone outside the firm, or beyond the people authorised to see it? ABA 512; California SB 574 (from 2027); DC Opinion 388; Texas Opinion 705; Washington AO 202505; Michigan AI FAQs Who can reach the model server. Ollama has no login of its own, so whatever can reach port 11434 can send prompts and read model names. From another machine on the network run curl http://<office-machine-ip>:11434/api/version; it should fail unless you meant to share it. On the machine itself, Ollama should listen on 127.0.0.1, not 0.0.0.0. How to check and fix.
Does the tool send anything home, share by default, or keep more than it needs? California Practical Guidance (2026); Oregon 2025-205; Missouri 2024-11; Georgia Generative AI Toolkit; Alabama FO 2026-01; NC 2024 FEO 1 Sharing, community features and chat history. Open WebUI ships with community sharing on; set ENABLE_COMMUNITY_SHARING=False unless you use it. Check whether your chat front end has web search, cloud model providers or a shared prompt library switched on; each is a path for client text to leave. Decide how long chat history is kept and who can export it; Alabama's opinion notes that prompts and interaction logs may be discoverable. Open WebUI settings.
Can people inside the firm read another matter's prompts without knowing they are a client's? ABA 512 (the intra-firm point); Missouri 2026-09; Minnesota column; ABA 512 and Texas 705 on self-learning tools Users, workspaces and memory. A local model does not learn from prompts, but the front end can keep them. List every account in Open WebUI or AnythingLLM, remove shared logins, and turn off new sign-ups once your accounts exist (in Open WebUI, the admin panel's general settings). Keep one workspace or knowledge base per matter. Switch off memories or long-term context features unless each user understands they persist. AnythingLLM 1.13.0 up to 1.16.2 handed an owner's private Memories to anonymous visitors of an embedded chat widget (details).
Can an AI agent act on its own: read mail, open files, send things? California Practical Guidance (2026: agents with access to email, document stores or client files need limited scope, monitoring and access controls); Oregon 2026-208 (lawyer approval before an agent acts); Alabama FO 2026-01 Tools, agents and connectors. List what the chat front end can call: Open WebUI tools and functions, AnythingLLM agent skills, MCP servers, connected mailboxes or drives. Remove what you do not use, limit each to the folders or matters it needs, and keep anything that sends, deletes or files behind a human click. Treat any web page or document the agent reads as untrusted input.
Does AI record, transcribe or answer anyone: clients, prospective clients, witnesses? NYC Bar 2025-6 (clients) and 2026-2 (everyone else: by default, don't record); Oregon 2026-208 (intake chatbots, Rule 1.18); Arizona Supreme Court committee's best practices; Missouri 2026-09 (depositions) Recordings, transcripts and chatbot logs. Find out whether your meeting platform's AI notes or recording start by default, and how to turn them off. For anything you do record or transcribe locally (speech-to-text in the chat front end, a transcription model on the office machine), know where the audio, transcript and summary files are stored, who can open them and when they are deleted. If a website chatbot takes enquiries, the same questions apply to what prospective clients type.
Is the tool kept secure with reasonable efforts? NC 2024 FEO 1 (Rule 1.6(c)); Virginia; Arizona State Bar guidance; Colorado Rule Change 2026(02) (discipline for violations that result from using technology); Illinois Supreme Court AI policy; Pennsylvania Joint Formal Opinion 2024-200; NYC Bar Formal Opinion 2024-5; Alaska 2025-1 Known vulnerabilities, encryption and logins. Type your Ollama, Open WebUI, AnythingLLM, vLLM or LiteLLM version into the CVE checker. Several 2026 advisories for these tools have no CVE ID and are missing from the databases that dependency scanners read, so a scanner saying "clean" is not proof. Turn on BitLocker or FileVault on the machine that holds the models and chat history, and keep the front end behind its own login. Treat any web content the tool reads as untrusted.
Do you still paste into a cloud assistant sometimes? Florida 24-1 (informed consent before a third-party tool sees confidential information); DC 388; Washington 202505; Ohio Ethics Guide; West Virginia L.E.O. 2024-01 Mask before you paste. Paste-Safe replaces names, organisations, emails and numbers with placeholders in your browser before text goes to ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini, and restores them in the reply. Nothing is uploaded. It reduces risk; it does not anonymise.
Are citations verified before filing? California SB 574 (every citation personally verified, from 2027); Pennsylvania 2024-200; Oregon 2025-205; New Mexico 2024-004; Kentucky KBA E-457; New Jersey Preliminary Guidelines; Illinois policy Local models hallucinate too. The public database kept by Damien Charlotin listed 2,184 decisions involving hallucinated material on 10 October 2026 (CC BY 4.0). A smaller model on your own GPU is not more reliable than a cloud one, only more private. Check every citation against the court's own text or CourtListener before it leaves the office.
Is there a written policy, and do clients know? Missouri 2024-11; New Jersey's 30 March 2026 Notice to the Bar (sample starter AI policy); New Jersey State Bar Association task force report (sample policy and vendor questions); Kentucky's sample AI use policy (April 2026); Illinois ARDC guide; NYSBA Task Force report; Hawaiʻi Chief Justice's letter to the bar (Appendix 4 of the committee report) Write down what you checked. Which machine, which tools and versions, who has accounts, what listens on the network, what agents and connectors are enabled, where recordings and chat history live and for how long, and the date. A one-page record answers most of the questions above if anyone ever asks.

A ten-minute self check

  1. Run the free, read-only local-ai-checkup script on the machine that hosts the model. It reports what is exposed, risky or slow and prints nothing secret.
  2. From a second machine on the office network, try the curl line above. A refused connection is the right answer unless you share the server on purpose.
  3. Put each tool's version into the CVE checker.
  4. Open the user list of your chat front end. Remove accounts you cannot name, turn off open sign-up, keep one workspace per matter, and turn off community sharing, memory features, tools and connectors you do not use.
  5. Write the answers down with today's date.
