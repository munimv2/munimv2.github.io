Inclusive eligibility check

Paste a clinical study's eligibility criteria. This page lists each exclusion item that touches disability (cognitive, consent capacity, vision, hearing, communication, mobility, and related items such as investigator discretion). It also lists each inclusion item that requires an ability, such as "able to walk 100 m unaided" or "normal hearing", because those shut people out too. For each item it gives an inclusive alternative to consider.

Your text stays on your computer. The check runs in this page, and nothing you paste is sent or saved anywhere.

Include the lines that start "Inclusion Criteria" and "Exclusion Criteria", as on ClinicalTrials.gov.

If an item matches the study's own subject (vision in a glaucoma trial, for example), the page says so, because that exclusion may be needed.

What a flag means

A flag means a criterion uses words linked to disability. It does not mean the criterion is wrong. Some exclusions and requirements are needed for safety or because the outcome depends on that function. For each flagged item, ask three questions. Would taking part be unsafe even with an accommodation? Does the outcome itself need this function? Or is the barrier only how the study is run, which an accommodation can fix? The inclusive eligibility guide works through each category.

The check uses word rules, not a reader. It misses criteria worded in ways it does not know, and it can flag a word used in passing. The same open rules are used by trial-exclusion-census to count disability-related criteria across every interventional study on ClinicalTrials.gov.
